嫦娥奔月

The Legend of Chang'e

One of China's most beloved moon legends, passed down through generations.

月宫里的嫦娥

According to the legend, Chang'e was the wife of the great archer Hou Yi. After Hou Yi received an elixir of immortality, he entrusted it to Chang'e for safekeeping.


One day, while Hou Yi was away, Chang'e drank the elixir. She became immortal and ascended toward the moon, where she would remain forever in the Moon Palace.


There, she is said to be accompanied by the Jade Rabbit, who endlessly prepares the elixir beneath the glowing moon.

Dalam legenda Tiongkok, Chang'e adalah istri pemanah hebat bernama Hou Yi. Setelah memperoleh ramuan keabadian, Hou Yi menitipkannya kepada Chang'e.


Ketika Hou Yi sedang pergi, Chang'e meminum ramuan tersebut. Ia menjadi abadi dan terbang menuju bulan, kemudian tinggal di Istana Bulan untuk selamanya.


Ia dipercaya ditemani oleh Kelinci Giok yang terus membuat ramuan keabadian di bawah cahaya bulan.

中秋的意义

The Spirit of Mid-Autumn

Beyond the moon and lanterns, the festival carries deep meanings that connect generations.

Reunion · 团圆

The full moon represents completeness and family reunion. Families gather together beneath the brightest moon of the year.

Light · 光明

Lanterns symbolize hope, guidance and the warmth of light during the autumn night.

Gratitude · 感恩

Sharing tea, food and stories is a beautiful way to express gratitude for family and togetherness.

月饼

The Mooncake Tradition

A golden pastry carrying centuries of history, symbolism and family memories.

A Golden Symbol of Reunion

Mooncakes are traditionally shared during the Mid-Autumn Festival. Their round shape reflects the full moon and symbolizes completeness, harmony and family reunion.

Over time, mooncakes have developed into many regional styles, fillings and artistic designs. Some are delicate and floral, while others carry intricate Chinese characters or traditional patterns on their surface.
